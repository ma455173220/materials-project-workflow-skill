# Topological invariants from symmetry eigenvalues

Apply this when a topological invariant is wanted and the crystal has symmetries whose eigenvalues at high-symmetry momenta diagnose it: parity criteria, irreducible representations and symmetry indicators. These methods use DFT wavefunctions at a few momenta and need no Wannier model; they complement Wilson loops ([Wannier guidance](wannier.md)) and do not replace them. Which methods to use and how to cross-check them are the researcher's choices; record them in `WORKFLOW.md`.

## 1. What symmetry eigenvalues can and cannot decide

- They diagnose only the invariants the eigenvalues fix. A nonzero indicator shows nontrivial topology of the band set below the chosen filling, provided that set has a direct gap to the bands above at every k. A zero indicator does not show triviality: some invariants are not indicated, topology can be fragile, and some groups have no nontrivial indicator. Many groups without inversion have no Z₂ indicator; there, use Wilson loops.
- Eigenvalues are read only at high-symmetry momenta, so a gap closing elsewhere is invisible; find the smallest direct gap on a dense mesh separately.
- Set the filling from the electron count of the calculation, every valence band of the pseudopotential or PAW dataset included, semicore as well: one band per electron with spin–orbit coupling; one spin-degenerate band per two electrons without it; each spin channel from its own electron count, analysed separately, in a collinear spin-polarised run. Never restrict the analysis to a window near the Fermi level.
- At each analysed momentum the last occupied and first unoccupied states must not be degenerate; otherwise no invariant is defined for that filling, and the degeneracy may be symmetry-enforced.
- Momenta such as kᵢ = 0 or π are reduced coordinates in the primitive reciprocal basis. Symmetry programs often use a standardised cell; a conventional cell or supercell folds the bands and changes weak indices, so convert to one primitive cell before comparing methods.

## 2. Parity criteria (inversion)

- **Fu–Kane Z₂** (inversion and time reversal with spin–orbit coupling). At each time-reversal-invariant momentum Γᵢ, δᵢ is the product of the inversion eigenvalues of the occupied Kramers pairs, one value per pair. In 2D, (−1)^ν = ∏ δᵢ over the four Γᵢ; in 3D the product over all eight gives the strong index ν₀, and the four Γᵢ with kₖ = π give the weak index νₖ.
- **z₄ with time reversal** (3D): z₄ = Σ (n⁺ − n⁻)/4 mod 4 over the eight Γᵢ, with n^± counting occupied states of parity ±1; ν₀ = z₄ mod 2. z₄ = 2 with vanishing weak indices indicates higher-order or crystalline topology, not triviality; with nonzero weak indices z₄ depends on the inversion centre. Sign conventions can exchange z₄ = 1 and 3, not ν₀.
- **Inversion centre**: shifting it by half a lattice vector flips the sign of δᵢ at half of the Γᵢ when the number of occupied pairs is odd; ν₀ and νₖ do not change. Compare individual δᵢ only for the same origin.
- **Degenerate sets larger than a Kramers pair**: use the trace of inversion over the whole set; d states with trace Tr P contain (d − Tr P)/2 odd states. A non-integer count points to a wrongly split set or a setting mismatch. In nonsymmorphic groups one set at a zone-boundary Γᵢ can contain both parities.
- **Without spin–orbit coupling**, parities (one per spin-degenerate band) only predict the Z₂ of the state that spin–orbit coupling would gap. It becomes a result only after a calculation with spin–orbit coupling confirms the gap and the occupied parities, which then give the invariant directly.
- **Antiferromagnets**: without time reversal Fu–Kane does not apply as stated. With inversion and S (time reversal combined with a fractional translation), the parity product over the four momenta of a plane on which S² = −1, one value per S-degenerate pair, gives the Z₂ of that plane.
- **Magnetic systems with inversion**: count the odd-parity occupied states summed over the eight inversion-invariant momenta. An odd total means the filling cannot be gapped (a metal or a Weyl semimetal; locate the crossings before naming it); a total ≡ 2 mod 4 with zero Chern number on every plane indicates an axion insulator. This is the magnetic z₄ indicator up to convention.
- **Chern parity**: in 2D, or on a plane that inversion maps onto itself, the product of the inversion eigenvalues of all occupied bands over its four inversion-invariant momenta gives (−1)^C.

## 3. Irreducible representations and symmetry indicators

Programs (for example irvsp, vasp2trace, qeirreps, IrRep) read DFT wavefunctions and return irreducible representations or characters at chosen momenta. They differ in supported pseudopotentials, magnetic groups, cell settings and in the classification service their output feeds; confirm these in the documentation before computing the wavefunctions.

1. Determine the space group or magnetic space group with a recorded tolerance, and put the cell in the setting and origin the program's tables expect; a mismatch gives wrong labels or failures, sometimes silently. Confirm from the log which group, operations and cell the program used.
2. Compute wavefunctions at the momenta the method needs (for indicators and topological quantum chemistry, the maximal momenta, zone-boundary ones included), with the settings of the self-consistent run.
3. For a 2D material in a vacuum cell, the program treats the vacuum direction as periodic. Use layer criteria on the plane k⊥ = 0, and identify the vacuum axis in the program's (possibly reordered) cell. Check that band energies at zero and half a period along the vacuum direction coincide; if not, the layers are still coupled. Do not read the layer invariant from a 3D indicator of the slab cell: its strong index is zero, the layer Z₂ appears as the weak index along the vacuum direction, and z₄ depends on whether the inversion centre lies in a layer or in the vacuum.
4. Check the output before interpreting: multiplicities of irreducible representations are non-negative integers (non-integers point to a wrong setting, unconverged states or a degenerate set split by the tolerance); the reported band range covers the whole occupied set; the filling lies in a gap at every analysed momentum.
5. Interpret in this order: compatibility relations violated along some line or plane mean the filling cannot be gapped (a symmetry-enforced semimetal) and no insulating invariant applies; relations satisfied with a nonzero indicator mean stable topology; zero indicators but a decomposition into elementary band representations that needs negative coefficients means fragile topology; a decomposition with non-negative coefficients leaves no topology the eigenvalues can detect. Invariants that are not indicated remain open and need Wilson loops. The decomposition's Wyckoff positions, empty sites included, can guide Wannier projections.

## 4. Combining with Wilson loops

- With inversion and time reversal, the parity criterion is cheap and uses the full DFT occupied set, so it makes a good first result; a Wilson loop then confirms it and shows the centre flow. Without inversion, Wilson loops are primary, with indicators added where the group has nontrivial ones.
- A program that reads Wannier models can compare the model's irreducible representations with those from DFT, which also checks the representation match in step 1 of the Wannier guidance.
- When methods disagree, check in this order: the occupied set; the cell and reciprocal basis; a direct gap closing away from high-symmetry momenta; identical spin–orbit and magnetic settings; degeneracy handling and filling at each momentum; Wilson-loop convergence and reference line.
- Report each invariant with its method, filling (band index), spin–orbit setting, cell and gap check, and whether the methods agree.
